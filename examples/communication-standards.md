# Operating Standard: Client and Internal Communication Timelines

This is a worked artifact, not the workflow description itself — it's what "Team Performance Infrastructure" and "Escalation and Complex Resolution" look like as an actual document a new hire could follow on day one, not just a paragraph in the README. Every figure below is illustrative, adapted from the general shape of an operating standard rather than lifted from a real one.


## Response commitments by client tier

Not every client relationship carries the same complexity, and promising one response standard across all of them either overpromises on the complex accounts or underpromises on the simple ones.


**Individual and routine accounts** (established relationship, standard product, no active structural change): a target response window measured in a small number of business days, tracked against a rolling median rather than a worst-case estimate.


**Family office, foundation, and corporate accounts** (higher complexity, multiple stakeholders, or a product mix spanning more than one offering): a noticeably longer, multi-week window for anything requiring structural review, revisited whenever the underlying process changes.


**Accounts under active due diligence or a custom-portfolio build** (a structure that adds required review steps beyond the standard process): a timeline held for internal planning purposes rather than promised externally, since it depends on how much of the standard process the structure can skip versus how much extra diligence a risk review adds back in.


The standard here isn't any specific day count — it's the practice of tracking real response data by tier and updating the promised timeline against that data on a regular cadence, instead of letting a stale number sit in an onboarding document indefinitely.


## When a process change makes the timeline unpredictable

The hardest communication problem isn't a slow process — it's a process that just changed and hasn't produced enough data yet to know its new speed. The standard here: when a new requirement (additional documentation, a new review step, a new sign-off) makes a previously-reliable timeline unreliable, stop promising the old number rather than defend it past its expiration date. Say plainly that a firm timeline can't be given yet, commit to monitoring the data, and set a date — a specific number of months out — to report back with a real number.


## Client communication cadence

**Standard communication:**


- Initial outreach to a newly assigned client within 1 business day.
- Follow-up on any open request on a fixed cadence, roughly weekly.
- If a client goes silent through several consecutive follow-ups on a request they initiated, escalate to the referring relationship holder (the advisor, referring partner, or account owner) and ask them to re-establish contact.
- If that escalation doesn't produce a response within a further short window, the open item is logged as stalled rather than left open indefinitely with no owner checking on it.
- All communication attempts and outstanding items are logged in the client record itself, not just in email, so the history is visible to anyone who picks up the relationship.
- Client emails get a same-day-or-next-day response.

**Time-sensitive requests:**


Assignment and initial outreach both happen within a business day — the same commitment as standard requests, but with no queue delay before the clock starts.


## Coverage workflow


- Officers out of office for an extended stretch transfer their full active book to a named backup before they leave.
- Officers out for a shorter stretch only transfer specific accounts that have an urgent, date-driven deadline landing during their absence.
- Regardless of how long someone is out, the team lead is the fallback point of contact for anything time-sensitive that surfaces while they're away.

## Internal communication standard


- Internal messages get a next-business-day response under normal volume.
- During a known seasonal peak (year-end giving, a major tax-driven deadline), that window is explicitly extended, and the extension is communicated to the team in advance, not discovered when someone's message goes unanswered.

## Why this belongs in the model

Field Readiness for Team Performance Infrastructure and Escalation and Complex Resolution isn't "we're responsive" — it's a documented standard specific enough that a new hire could follow it without asking their manager what "usually" happens. A tiered response commitment, a defined escalation ladder, a coverage plan, and a rule for what to say when a timeline becomes genuinely unpredictable are what turn a workflow description into something the team can actually be held to.
