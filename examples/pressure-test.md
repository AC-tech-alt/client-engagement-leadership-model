# Pressure Test: A Cross-Segment Escalation Through the Model

The five-workflow model in the main README is easy to validate against a clean, single-segment relationship. The real test is whether it holds up when one client hits three workflows at once. This is a theoretical scenario built specifically to stress it, and an honest accounting of where the model needed to bend.


## The scenario

A single-family office client wants to convert its existing donor-advised fund into a custom portfolio and, in the same conversation, requests back-office administration for a private foundation the family is standing up. Both moves need to close inside three weeks to align with the family's tax year-end. Midway through, the due diligence team flags a concern about a fund manager the client specifically requested for the custom portfolio, putting the timeline and the relationship in direct tension.

This scenario is designed to hit the model in three places at once: a hard external deadline, a product conversion spanning two service lines, and a due-diligence dispute that the client experiences as a delay rather than a safeguard.


## Running it through the five Workflows

### 1. Team Performance Infrastructure

The KPI framework only helps here if it tracks relationships that span two products at once. A dashboard built around single-product accounts has no field for "DAF converting to custom portfolio while a new foundation stands up back-office administration," so this client either gets logged twice under two disconnected records or gets dropped from the metrics entirely. The framework needs a multi-product relationship as a first-class case, not an edge case discovered when one shows up.


### 2. Escalation and Complex Resolution

This is where the pressure is highest. The due-diligence concern is legitimate and shouldn't be waived to hit a deadline, but the tiered escalation path only works if it can move at the speed of a three-week client deadline, not the speed of a standard review cycle. The model's answer isn't to skip diligence to save the timeline - it's to have an expedited-review path already defined, with a named owner who can pull the due diligence team into a compressed conversation instead of a queue.


### 3. Relationship Engagement Planning

If this client's engagement plan had already flagged the family's likely move toward a custom portfolio and a new foundation, the three-week deadline wouldn't be a surprise - it would be the plan's own timeline arriving. The model holds here only if engagement plans capture forward-looking product trajectory, not just current-state account status.


### 4. Team Development


This scenario tests whether the officer of record has the standing to work across product lines, or whether the client gets handed between a DAF specialist and a custom-portfolio specialist mid-crisis. Team development has to build officers who can carry a relationship across a product conversion, not just within one product silo, or a cross-segment moment like this one becomes a handoff failure on top of everything else.


### 5. Cross-functional Voice of the Client

Two teams outside client engagement - due diligence and back-office administration - both need to be pulled into the same three-week window, on the same client, at the same time. If the voice-of-the-client channel only routes feedback in the slow, aggregate, quarterly sense, it's useless here. This scenario needs a live, working channel that can put both teams in a room with the account owner this week, not a mechanism for surfacing patterns after the fact.


## Where the model held, and where it needed to bend

It held: the five-workflow separation itself. Keeping team performance, escalation, engagement planning, team development, and cross-functional partnership as distinct trackable pieces - rather than one collapsed "the client is handled" status - is what let a three-front problem get worked as three visible problems instead of one confusing one.

It needed to bend: the model as written in the main README assumes a client sits in one segment with one product track. Under this scenario, both the KPI framework and the engagement-planning workflow needed a second dimension - a client moving across products and segments at once - built in from the start, not patched on when the first multi-product client showed up. A model that only works for single-product relationships isn't a real client engagement framework; it's a description of the easy case.


## What this means for where I'd start

The "Team Performance Infrastructure first" sequencing in the main README still holds, with one addition: the KPI framework and the escalation path both need to be built to handle multi-product, multi-segment relationships from day one. If that capability gets treated as phase-two work, the first client who converts products under a deadline forces it into existence under pressure - the exact failure mode Workflow 1 was supposed to prevent.
